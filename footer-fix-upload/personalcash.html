Empower Personal Cash

A full redesign introducing goal tracking and emergency fund creation features to Empower's cash account, turning a passive holding account into something users actively use.

Role
Design Lead / Sole Product Designer
Timeline
18 months (Jan 2025 – Jun 2026)
Team
2 Product Managers, UX writer, Business Analyst, 2 Engineering teams (web/mobile)
The redesign at a glance
Before — static transaction ledger
After — goal-oriented account dashboard
Before — mobile, no goal tracking
After — Emergency Fund & goals front and center

The problem

Personal Cash was Empower's least-engaged product. User research surfaced a startling statistic: about half of users who opened a Cash account ever successfully deposited money into it. Half the funnel evaporated after account creation.

46%
of users who opened a Cash account never successfully deposited money into it

Our assumption was that gap was a motivation problem, and so we went after the reasons people keep money in their accounts.

The proposal

Along with restructuring the deposit flow, we proposed reframing the account to focus around what people were ACTUALLY saving for. 

1
Ask for the deposit first
Move the deposit-amount question to the start of onboarding, capturing intent while motivation is highest rather than after setup is done.
2
Goals inside the account
Allow users to create savings goals with a target amount, due date, and progress indicator, so users are motivated to deposit $$$ toward something instead of an idle number.
3
Emergency fund as the first goal
A guided flow with a recommended target, so users who don't know what to save for still have an obvious, low-pressure place to start.
HYPOTHESIS
If users open a Cash account with a named goal and a committed deposit amount, more accounts get funded — and funded accounts keep receiving deposits.

Estimated impact. We framed success around the funnel gap we could already measure, not vanity metrics:

  • Primary: Deposit conversion. Close the gap between accounts opened and accounts actually funded (the 46% who never deposited).
  • Secondary: Recurring deposits. Goal progress gives a reason to return, so we expected recurring and one-time deposits per funded account to rise.
  • Secondary: Onboarding completion. Research testing already showed the upfront deposit ask lifted both account-creation completion and deposit success.
  • Directional: Balance retention. Money earmarked for a goal is money less likely to be withdrawn shortly after deposit.

Process

Competitive analysis. I audited how comparable fintech products handled goal tracking and savings, collecting UI patterns across Monzo, Chase, Citibank, Wealthfront, and Ally to ground our ideation in what already worked in the market.

Requirements definition. Working closely with the PM and decision-making team, I translated the research and business hypothesis into validated product requirements for goal creation and emergency fund tracking.

Research-led design. Empower's User Research team ran the initial testing, and their findings directly shaped the design. The most consequential insight: asking users how much they wanted to deposit at the very beginning of onboarding, rather than after account setup, improved both account creation completion and deposit success rates.

Design system alignment. I refined the UI to match Empower's latest design system, so the new flows felt native rather than bolted on.

Funnel optimization. I mapped the different entry points into Personal Cash and redesigned each funnel around the early-deposit-ask insight and the new goal features.

Handoff. I partnered with the mobile and web dev teams to translate final designs and requirements into buildable specs.

Navigating a moving team

Two product managers cycled through this project. The first left the company roughly halfway through — and for about three months there was no PM at all. During that gap, I stepped into much of the PM function: communicating directly with the UX writer, business analyst, and dev teams, defining and validating requirements, and catching handoff issues before they became blockers. When the second PM came on, I used my accumulated context to bring them up to speed while development was still in motion.

What shipped

v1.0 of the redesigned Empower Personal Cash:

  • Goal creation and tracking tied directly to the Cash account
  • Emergency fund setup as a distinct, guided flow
  • Reworked onboarding that asks for a deposit amount upfront
  • Optimized entry-point funnels across mobile and web

What the work established

As the sole designer, I translated that direction into web and mobile flows and worked with engineering through handoff. When the project went roughly three months without a PM, I also helped clarify requirements, resolve open questions, and preserve the reasoning behind our decisions as the team changed.

In addition, the goal-tracking framework became an internal reference for exploring the concept of goals across other Empower products. That was a meaningful extension of the project’s influence, although I don’t have post-launch metrics confirming an increase in funded accounts or repeat deposits.

Goals expanding beyond Personal Cash: goal-tracking modules surfacing on Empower's main account Overview page.

Reflection

Personal Cash expanded my ownership beyond design: I helped define requirements and kept web and mobile work moving through a 3 month PM gap. The goal-tracking work also informed broader product exploration at Empower. While its business impact remains yet to be unverified as it just went live, the ideal next step I'd take would be to test whether connecting savings to a goal actually helped more customers fund their accounts.
